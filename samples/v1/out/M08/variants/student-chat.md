同类题 1

Leo had played the violin in the school orchestra for a year, yet few people noticed him. During breaks he would wander off alone, listening closely to the hall's empty corners. A month before the spring concert, the music teacher complained that the brass section always sounded muddy. The players practised harder, but nothing changed. Leo finally suggested a simple test: he moved the brass chairs two metres away from the back wall. Their sound immediately became clearer, because the wall had been bouncing it back too early. On concert night, the teacher asked Leo to explain the new seating to the audience. "Sometimes," she said, "the best listener in the room solves the problem."

1\. Why did the brass section sound muddy?

A. The players did not practise enough.    B. The back wall reflected their sound too early.    C. Their instruments were old.    D. The hall was too small.

2\. What can we infer about Leo?

A. He paid careful attention to how sound behaved.    B. He wanted to leave the orchestra.    C. He led the brass section.    D. He found practice boring.

3\. What does the teacher mean by her last words?

A. Careful listening can lead to solutions.    B. Only teachers can solve problems.    C. Audiences should stay quiet.    D. Musicians must speak more often.

同类题 2

Sara joined the school newspaper as a photographer and seldom offered opinions in meetings. While the writers rushed to finish stories, she kept a small table of every number the paper printed. Before the issue about the canteen survey went out, she noticed something odd: the article said 70 percent of students liked the new menu, but only 120 of the 300 forms said so. The editor checked the forms and found that the writer had counted the same pile twice. The story was corrected just in time. At the end of the term, the editor gave Sara a new role. "Checking facts," he explained, "is as important as telling stories."

1\. What was wrong with the canteen article?

A. It used an old photo.    B. Its percentage did not match the forms.    C. It was printed too late.    D. It criticised the cooks.

2\. What can we infer about Sara?

A. She was careful with details.    B. She wanted to be a writer.    C. She disliked the new menu.    D. She often argued with the editor.

3\. What new role was Sara most probably given?

A. Photographer.    B. Fact checker.    C. Canteen manager.    D. Story writer.

同类题 3

Tom was the youngest member of the town's tree-planting club, and he mostly listened during planning meetings. Last autumn, half of the young trees the club planted along the river died within weeks. The older members thought the soil was too poor and wanted to buy expensive fertiliser. Tom, who walked along the river every morning, had noticed that the dead trees were all on the side where people let their dogs run. He suggested putting up simple wooden fences instead. The club tried his idea on twenty new trees, and every one of them survived the winter. At the spring meeting, the chairwoman asked Tom to lead the next planting day.

1\. Why did many young trees die?

A. The soil had no fertiliser.    B. Dogs damaged them on one side of the river.    C. The winter was too cold.    D. They were planted too late.

2\. What can we infer about Tom?

A. He noticed things others missed.    B. He knew a lot about fertiliser.    C. He owned several dogs.    D. He disliked meetings.

3\. Why did the chairwoman ask Tom to lead the next planting day?

A. He was the oldest member.    B. His idea had proved effective.    C. He had bought the fences.    D. He asked for the job.
